# Interactive-aquarium-product-idea

## Today's Product Innovation Journey – Project PetPals 2026 - Prodlab IIMI's Product Freshers competition 

## 1. The competition

Participated in Product Freshers' 2026, under Project PetPals. The challenge was to identify an underserved problem in the pet-care ecosystem and propose an innovative product. We were assigned fish as the pet. 

Teammates - Ayush Agarwal, Riya Jha

## 2. My idea: AquaPlay – An Interactive Aquarium

**Core insight:** Aquariums are often treated as decorative installations rather than interactive experiences. Think about aquariums in restaurants and hotels: people, especially children, look at the fish for a few seconds, perhaps tap the glass, and walk away.

Fish are largely passive pets from the human interaction perspective. Unlike dogs or cats, there are limited ways for owners and visitors to actively engage with them.

I wanted to change this by making fish more interesting pets and improving human–fish interaction.

### Core product concept

AquaPlay is an interactive aquarium with an external physical control panel that allows visitors, particularly children, to modify elements of the aquarium environment.

**Three primary features:**

1. **Movable underwater maze:** Motor-controlled plastic/acrylic partitions or walls inside the aquarium can change positions through buttons on an external control panel, creating different swimming routes and configurations.

2. **Dynamic aquarium wall screens:** Screen-like displays integrated into the aquarium walls can change the visual environment, such as coral reefs, volcanic underwater landscapes, kelp forests and underwater cities.

3. **Pepper's Ghost hologram:** A holographic-style optical display in one corner of the aquarium, potentially displaying an animated octopus or other underwater creatures.

### Additional/optional features explored

- Motion-sensor or fish-tracking LED tunnels that glow when fish swim through them.
- Transparent overhead pipes connecting different sections of the same aquarium.
- Movable mirrors for changing visual effects.
- Camera-based fish tracking and interactive lighting.

### Target market

Primarily B2B:
- Restaurants with decorative aquariums.
- Hotels and hospitality establishments.
- Potentially museums, schools and premium residential aquariums.

The objective is to turn an overlooked decorative aquarium into an attraction that encourages visitors to stop, interact and engage.

The fish would also get opportunities for voluntary exploration and environmental enrichment, although actual welfare benefits would need testing.

## 3. Engineering exploration

I explored possible implementation approaches:

- Motors and actuators located outside the aquarium.
- Mechanical movement transmitted to internal plastic/acrylic structures.
- Magnetic coupling as one possible way to transfer movement through aquarium glass.
- Rounded edges, guarded mechanisms and fail-safe configurations to minimise fish injury.
- Raspberry Pi + OpenCV for camera-based fish detection.
- ESP32 for controlling LEDs, motors and other electronics.

I also developed a working HTML/JavaScript simulation using ChatGPT:
- Randomly swimming fish.
- Independently movable maze partitions.
- Multiple underwater screen themes.
- Animated octopus hologram.
- External control panel with interactive buttons.

This helped turn the idea into something demonstrable rather than just a verbal concept.

## 4. Business model explored

Indicative B2B selling price: ₹15k per installation, excluding the existing aquarium.

Potential revenue streams:
- Hardware and installation.
- Annual maintenance.
- Custom visual themes and feature upgrades.

This was an early pricing hypothesis, not a validated cost estimate.
